# Activity 04 · Your profile README, with your project pinned

Your profile page is the first thing anyone sees when they open your GitHub link. GitHub shows a README at the top of it if you create one special repository: one named exactly as your username. You write a short profile that points to your project, then pin the project so it sits right under the README.

| Step | What the reader needs | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A README on your profile at all | The special repository |
| 2 | Who you are, in two lines | Headline and about |
| 3 | Your work and how to reach you | Projects, skills, contact |
| 4 | The project itself, one click away | A description and a pin |

## 1. Create the special repository

**Why this step.** GitHub shows a profile README only when four things hold: the repository has your exact username as its name, it is public, it has a `README.md` at its top level, and that file has some content.

1. Open **+ → New repository**.
2. In **Repository name**, type your username exactly, with the same letters and hyphens.
3. Choose **Public** and turn on **Add a README file**. Click **Create repository**.

**C1.** Before you click **Create repository**, GitHub shows a message under the name saying this is a special repository whose README appears on your profile. If you see no message, the name does not match your username: check the spelling.

## 2. Headline and about

**Why this step.** A reader decides in a few seconds whether to keep reading. One line says what you are; two sentences say what you do. Skills and projects come next.

1. Open `README.md` in the new repository and click the pencil icon. Delete its text.
2. Open the [profile template](../Resources/profile-template.md), click **Raw**, copy it all and paste it in.
3. Replace `Your Name` with your name. Replace the capitals after the first sentence with one sentence of your own.

**C2.** In **Preview**: your name as a large heading, then **Data analyst** in bold, then your two sentences.

## 3. Projects, skills and contact

**Why this step.** The project links are the reason a profile exists: they lead to evidence. Give both links, the repository for readers who want the SQL and the website for those who do not.

1. In the **Projects** line, replace both `your-username` with your username, and the capitals with your finding in one line.
2. Keep only the skills you can show in a project. Delete any line you cannot back up.
3. Put in your LinkedIn address and the email you want employers to use, or delete the lines you do not want public.
4. **Commit changes…**, message `Write my profile README`, **Commit changes**.

**C3.** Open `github.com/your-username`: your README is at the top of your profile. Click both project links: the first opens your repository, the second your website (if it is not ready yet, it shows a 404 page; try again in a few minutes).

## 4. A description and a pin

**Why this step.** Pinned repositories sit right under the README, with their description. The description is often the only line a visitor reads about a project, so it should carry the finding too.

1. Open `austin-bikes-2023`. Beside **About** on the right, click the gear icon. Rewrite the **Description** as your finding in one short line, and click **Save changes**.
2. Open your profile, `github.com/your-username`. Above your repositories, click **Customize your pins**, tick `austin-bikes-2023`, and click **Save pins**.

**C4.** Your profile shows your README, then a **Pinned** box with `austin-bikes-2023` and your one-line description.

**What just happened.** Your GitHub link now leads somewhere: a short profile, then a pinned project whose first screen shows what you found. Post both links in Slack: `github.com/your-username` and `your-username.github.io/austin-bikes-2023/`.
